# QUALITY BAR — Bandicoot Bonanza

The visual spec, the floors, and the loop that closes the gap between them.

This document is adapted from the `PROJECT_LAAS_v2.md` / `CITY-QUALITY-BAR.md`
discipline used to build the Far Country 3D world: a stated bar, six pillars,
numeric floors, a ranked delta list, and a banned-outcomes list that makes
failure legible instead of arguable. What is **not** carried over is that
project's photoreal target and its engine architecture. Those were calibrated
for a WebGPU/TSL renderer with GI probes and 5M-triangle hero shots. This is a
single HTML file on Three.js r128 WebGL. The method transfers; the numbers do
not, and pretending otherwise would produce a spec nobody could hit.

---

## The bar

The visual target is **current-generation AAA stylized mascot platformer** —
the class of image *Crash Bandicoot 4: It's About Time*, *Astro Bot*, *Sackboy:
A Big Adventure*, and *Ratchet & Clank: Rift Apart* produce. Specifically:

- **Saturated, art-directed colour** with a deliberate script per biome, not
  "some greens and some browns."
- **Chunky, readable, over-articulated shapes.** Every prop is *designed* —
  bevels, trim, banding, wear. Nothing is a raw primitive.
- **Dense set dressing.** The play corridor sits inside a world that continues
  past it; the corridor is never the only thing in frame.
- **Soft, coloured light.** Shadow is blue-green and full of bounce, never grey
  and never black. Rim light separates the hero from the background at all
  times.
- **Everything is grounded.** Contact shadows, occlusion in every crevice, and
  props that meet the ground rather than intersecting it.

You will not fully reach it — that is expected, and the constraints below are
real. The task is to close as much of the gap as a single-file WebGL build
allows and to *know precisely* how far you got. What is not acceptable is
building to a lower bar because the lower bar is comfortable.

A result that looks like **a Three.js primitives demo** — untextured spheres
and cones, grey shadow, flat slabs floating over a plane, props with no
thickness — is a **failed task**, no matter how good the game feel is. That
is, verified by screenshot in [`DELTA.md`](DELTA.md), what this build looked
like on 2026-08-19.

### On reference images

Far Country ships its reference frames in `reference-city/`; those are
photographs. The equivalent references here would be screenshots of shipped
commercial games, which this repo will not redistribute. The bar above is
therefore written out in words, and the delta loop judges shots against the
**floors** in this document rather than against a side-by-side.

If you want a true side-by-side, drop your own reference frames in `refs/`
(gitignored) and use `node tools/compare.mjs --a <shot> --b refs/<ref>`.

## Fixed constraints — these do not move

| Constraint | Value |
|---|---|
| Delivery | **One `index.html`.** No build step, no bundler, no install. Open it and play. |
| Assets | **Zero external assets.** Every mesh, texture and sound is generated by the code or baked into the file. |
| Renderer | Three.js **r128, WebGL** from a pinned CDN, with `EffectComposer`, `UnrealBloomPass` and `FXAA` from the matching examples build. |
| Determinism | `?seed=N` reproduces the entire world. |
| Fallback | `?minfx` disables shadows and post for software renderers. It may look worse; it may not look broken. |

**The one-file rule is a deliberate inversion of the source discipline.**
`PROJECT_LAAS_v2.md` lists "one-file architecture" as an instant fail, because
that project is a 20k-LOC engine. Here the single file *is* the product — the
README promises it and the game's whole premise rests on it. Section banners
(`/* ===== 7. PARTICLES ===== */`) do the work module boundaries do there.
Anything that would require a build step is out of scope by definition, not by
preference.

## The six pillars

Every requirement below serves one of these. If a decision comes up that this
document does not cover, resolve it in favour of the pillar.

**A. Designed geometry, not primitives.** Detail lives in silhouette and
surface. A crate has chamfered edges, corner brackets and plank relief. A palm
frond droops and has depth. A fern is fronds, not a cone. *Rule: no object
within 12 m of a review camera may read as an unmodified Three.js primitive.
If you can name the constructor from the screenshot, it fails.*

**B. Coloured light, no grey shadow.** The toon ramp is the lighting model
here, so the ramp itself carries the art direction: shadow is cool and
saturated (blue-green under canopy, blue-violet on stone), light is warm.
Shadowed faces show bounce from whatever is next to them. *Rule: sample any
shadowed pixel — if its saturation is below ~0.15, lighting has failed.
`tools/compare.mjs --sample` exists to check exactly this.*

**C. Nothing is bare.** Every surface class has occupants. Sand gets shells,
pebbles, driftwood, ripple relief and footprint-scale debris. Moss gets ferns,
flowers, roots, fallen leaves. Stone gets cracks, moss creep, rubble at the
base. Platform edges get trim. *Rule: in a ground-level shot, untextured or
undressed ground may not occupy a large share of frame within 12 m.*

**D. Distance holds, and the world is not a diorama.** The play corridor sits
in a world that continues past it: sea stacks, a jungle mass, a temple
silhouette, an island shelf under the platforms. Platforms have foundations —
cliff, root mass, stilts, something. *Rule: no framing may show a play surface
whose underside is a bare extruded slab hanging in air. Fog may grade the
distance; it may not be the reason there is nothing there.*

**E. Art direction.** A colour script per biome (beach → jungle → temple),
enforced through fog, light colour, and the grade — the existing `FOG_STOPS`
lerp is the right instinct and needs the rest of the palette to follow it.
Composed hero framings, not found ones. Bloom serves the art; it does not
erase the shape of everything it touches.

**F. The world moves.** Wind runs through every plant from one shared field,
not per-object phases that agree with nothing. Water flows and breaks at its
margin. Torch light flickers on the surfaces around it, not only on itself.
A frozen frame should still feel one second away from motion.

## Floors

Measured against the 2026-08-19 baseline at 1280×800 (`docs/shots/`), which
ran **33.6k–41.5k triangles and 119–321 draw calls** per framing. The
triangle floors below are roughly a 6× increase; that is the correct order of
magnitude for the gap between "primitives demo" and the stated bar, and it is
comfortably inside what instanced WebGL geometry does at 60 fps.

| Dimension | Floor |
|---|---|
| **Rendered triangles** | **≥ 220k** in ground-level hero framings, **≥ 150k** in aerials (per-frame, all composer passes, HUD-verified). Ceiling: whatever holds 60 fps on a mid GPU. |
| **Draw calls** | ≤ 260 per frame. Growth in detail must come from instancing and merged geometry, not from more `scene.add`. |
| Hero character | Distinct silhouette at portrait range: markings, ears, brow, muzzle, tail and paw shapes readable; a visible outline; a contact shadow under the feet in every framing. |
| Prop geometry | Crates, TNT, totems, torches, bounce pads and the gate each carry modelled relief (bevel, trim, bracket, inset). No prop is a single untrimmed box. |
| Ground cover | ≥ 3 plant species with real frond/blade geometry and per-instance hue, scale and lean variation; ≥ 2 debris classes (pebbles, shells, twigs, leaf litter) at ≥ 1,500 visible instances in dressed zones. |
| Platform foundations | Every play surface has a modelled underside: cliff face, root mass, stilts or rubble skirt. No bare extruded slab in any framing. |
| Backdrop | ≥ 3 distinct distance layers (near dressing, mid mass, far silhouette) visible in every outdoor framing. |
| Shadow colour | Toon ramp is chromatic. Sampled shadow saturation ≥ 0.15; sampled shadow value > 0 (nothing crushes to black). |
| Water | Depth-graded colour, a shoreline band, foam at contact, and visible flow. Not a single translucent plane. |
| Texture variation | Every repeating texture carries a macro breakup layer at a different frequency from its detail, so tiling is not readable at gameplay distance. |
| Emissive control | Bloom may not erase the shape of the object it blooms. Fruit, gems and flames must still read as forms, not as white blobs. |
| Motion | One shared wind field drives all vegetation. No hero element in a framing is fully static. |

## Banned outcomes — instant fail

- Grey or black shadow. Desaturated shadow terminator on any surface.
- Raw Three.js primitives in frame: recognisable cones as plants, spheres as
  fruit, untrimmed boxes as props, dodecahedrons as rocks.
- Play surfaces floating as bare slabs over a plane; props intersecting the
  ground instead of meeting it.
- Cloned vegetation — shared mesh varied only by rotation and scale.
- Bloom blowing an emissive object out to a featureless white blob.
- Visible texture tiling at gameplay distance; single-frequency noise as a
  material's only detail.
- Fog used as the reason there is nothing in the distance.
- A backdrop that is only sky and one flat water plane.
- Triangle counts below the floors above (under-rendering the GPU).
- Adding a build step, an external asset, or a runtime dependency.

## The loop

**Reference-delta loop (mandatory, every pass):**

1. `node tools/shots.mjs --dir shots/before`
2. Read `shots/before/CONTACT-SHEET.png`. Write the **ten most visually
   significant gaps against this document**, ranked by impact, into
   [`DELTA.md`](DELTA.md).
3. Fix the top three.
4. `node tools/shots.mjs --dir shots/after` and compare.
5. Only then does the pass close. Move the fixed entries to the delta's
   closed section with the date and what actually changed.

Framings are owned code (`index.html` section 14b), not coordinates in this
file — that is the lesson `CITY-QUALITY-BAR.md` learned the hard way when its
written-down poses silently stopped framing the thing they were judging.

### Self-score rubric

Per row: 10 = passes a one-second glance beside a shipped AAA stylized
platformer at 1080p; 7 = clearly a browser game but the same *class* of image;
4 = good hobby demo; 2 = engine primitives demo. Score after every pass; for
each row write "what raises this by 2 points" and implement the two cheapest
before continuing.

Rows: character fidelity · prop design · ground dressing · vegetation ·
light transport · colour script · water · distance and backdrop · motion ·
composition · performance.
